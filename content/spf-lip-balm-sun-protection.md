Sunscreen often stops at the edge of the face, leaving the lips unprotected. Lip tissue is thinner and the lower lip receives substantial ultraviolet exposure. Sunburn can cause soreness, swelling and peeling, while years of exposure can contribute to actinic cheilitis—a precancerous form of lip damage.

## ☀️ Choose Broad-Spectrum SPF 30+

Look for a lip product explicitly labeled **broad spectrum SPF 30 or higher**. A comfortable product is important because protection only works when you use enough and reapply. Zinc oxide or titanium dioxide may suit some sensitive lips, although any formula can irritate an individual.

Do not assume lipstick, gloss or ordinary balm provides UV protection unless the label gives an SPF and broad-spectrum claim.

## ⏰ Reapply Like Sunscreen

Apply before going outdoors. Reapply about every two hours and after eating, drinking, swimming, sweating or wiping the mouth. A morning application cannot reliably protect through lunch and an afternoon commute.

Use broad-spectrum sunscreen on the surrounding face and seek shade when possible.

## 👒 Build Layers of Protection

A wide-brimmed hat, shade and avoiding unnecessary midday exposure reduce the amount of UV reaching the lips. Water, snow and sand can increase reflected exposure. Outdoor workers and people who spend long hours driving should make lip reapplication part of the routine.

Moisturize dry surrounding skin so cracking does not tempt you to lick or pick.

## 🔥 If Your Lips Are Already Sunburned

Move out of the sun, use cool—not icy—compresses and apply a bland non-irritating lip moisturizer. Do not peel skin or apply exfoliating acids. Severe blistering, major swelling, dehydration symptoms or signs of infection need medical advice.

Wash the surrounding face gently after sweating or swimming rather than scrubbing.

## 🚩 Know the Chronic-Damage Signs

Arrange a dermatology assessment for a persistently rough, scaly or discolored lip; loss of the clear border between lip and skin; recurrent crusting; a tender thick spot; or an ulcer or lump that does not heal. These changes should not be treated as ordinary dryness indefinitely.

## ❓ Frequently Asked Questions

### Does darker lip color prevent sun damage?

Natural pigment offers some protection but does not eliminate UV damage. Every skin tone benefits from lip sun protection.

### Can I put facial sunscreen directly on my lips?

Use a product whose label includes lip use. Products not intended for ingestion or the lip surface may taste unpleasant or irritate.

### Is SPF 15 enough?

AAD guidance recommends SPF 30 or higher for lips.

## ✅ Bottom Line

Lip sunscreen is not a beach-only product. Choose broad-spectrum SPF 30+, reapply, use shade and get persistent lip changes checked.
