Brittle nails can split lengthwise, peel in thin layers or break before they grow. That does not automatically mean you have a vitamin deficiency. Repeated water exposure, cleaning chemicals, polish removal, artificial nails, dry weather and normal aging are common contributors.

The useful question is not simply “Which supplement should I buy?” It is **what keeps drying, thinning or injuring the nail plate?** Remove that stress first, then give new nail time to grow.

## 🔎 Look for the Pattern

If most fingernails are affected and your hands spend hours in water, wet work is a strong clue. Nails repeatedly swell with water and contract as they dry, which can encourage splitting. If one nail changed after it was crushed, picked or aggressively filed, local trauma is more likely.

Sudden brittleness across many nails can sometimes accompany skin disease, iron deficiency, thyroid problems, medication effects or major nutritional change. A clinician—not an online photo—should investigate when the change is unexplained or comes with fatigue, hair loss, rash, pain or other symptoms.

## 🧤 Reduce Water and Chemical Exposure

Keep nails practical in length and wear suitable gloves for dishes, cleaning and other wet jobs. Cotton-lined household gloves may feel more comfortable for longer tasks. Avoid using fingernails to open cans, scrape labels or pry objects.

Wash with lukewarm water and a mild cleanser when needed, then dry carefully around the nail folds.

## 💧 Moisturize Nails as Well as Hands

Apply a rich fragrance-free cream after washing, rubbing it over the nail plate, sidewalls and cuticle. At night, a bland ointment can reduce water loss. This does not “feed” a dead nail plate, but it can improve flexibility and protect the surrounding barrier while healthier nail grows forward.

## 💅 Pause the Damage Cycle

Take a break from gel, dip and acrylic systems if nails are thin or peeling. Do not buff away layers to make the surface look smooth, and never peel off polish or enhancements. Use remover only as directed and limit skin contact.

If you return to UV-cured manicures, protect exposed hand skin with broad-spectrum, water-resistant SPF 30+ or opaque fingerless gloves designed for manicure use. Sunscreen does not prevent nail thinning from filing or acetone.

## ⏳ Expect Slow Improvement

Fingernails require months to replace themselves. Photograph one nail every four weeks in similar light instead of judging it daily. Improvement should appear as smoother, stronger growth near the cuticle that gradually moves toward the tip.

## ❓ Frequently Asked Questions

### Should I take biotin for brittle nails?

Evidence in healthy people is limited, and high-dose biotin can interfere with important laboratory tests. Discuss unexplained brittleness and any supplement with a clinician first.

### Does drinking more water fix brittle nails?

Normal hydration supports health, but drinking excessive water will not undo repeated chemical or mechanical damage.

### When should I see a dermatologist?

Book an assessment for pain, swelling, nail lifting, marked discoloration, a new dark streak, spooning, deep horizontal grooves or persistent change without an obvious cause.

## ✅ Bottom Line

Brittle nails usually need less water, less trauma and more barrier protection—not a complicated nail-growth routine. Protect your hands, moisturize consistently and allow several months for new growth.
