Cuticles are not useless overgrowth. This thin seal helps protect the space between the nail plate and surrounding skin. Cutting, tearing or repeatedly pushing it back can create an entry point for irritants and germs.

If your cuticles look rough, the safest goal is not to remove them. It is to reduce dryness, friction and picking while keeping the nail folds comfortable.

## 💧 Moisturize After Every Wash

Pat hands dry, then massage a fragrance-free cream over fingertips, nail folds and nail plates. At bedtime, a thin layer of plain petrolatum or another bland ointment can soften rough edges and reduce water loss.

You do not need a strongly fragranced “nail food.” The formula touching sensitive nail-fold skin matters more than the marketing name.

## ✂️ Leave the Living Seal Alone

Do not cut or forcefully push back attached cuticle. If a loose hangnail catches, wash your hands, use clean clippers to trim only the detached piece and avoid pulling. Never share clippers, and disinfect tools regularly.

Choose salons that clean tools between clients. Ask the technician to leave your cuticles alone and avoid any service that causes pain or bleeding.

## 🧤 Protect Against “Wet Work”

Dishes, cleaning sprays and repeated handwashing can inflame the skin around nails. Wear suitable gloves for household chemicals, remove them if sweat builds up and moisturize afterward. Use a mild cleanser rather than scrubbing nail folds with a brush.

## 🚨 Know Infection and Allergy Clues

Tender redness and swelling around a nail may be paronychia, an infection of the nail fold. Pus, spreading redness, severe throbbing, fever or worsening pain needs prompt medical advice. People with diabetes, poor circulation or reduced immunity should seek care early rather than performing home procedures.

Itchy, blistered or scaly skin after gel, acrylic or nail glue may be allergic contact dermatitis. Stop the suspected product and see a clinician; repeated exposure can worsen allergy. “HEMA-free” or “hypoallergenic” labels do not guarantee that a product is safe for you.

## ☀️ Protect the Backs of Hands

Hands receive cumulative UV exposure, and gel curing lamps add more. Before a UV-cured service, the AAD advises broad-spectrum, water-resistant SPF 30+ on exposed hands or dark, opaque fingerless gloves. Apply sunscreen to skin, not beneath the product on the nail plate.

## ❓ Frequently Asked Questions

### Is cuticle oil necessary?

No. It can improve flexibility, but a tolerated fragrance-free cream or plain petrolatum is also useful.

### Can I remove the white dead skin?

Do not guess what is living cuticle. Soften with moisturizer and trim only a clearly detached hangnail with clean tools.

### Why do cuticles keep growing rough after trimming?

Cutting can create irritation and a repeated rough-growth cycle. Leave them alone consistently and protect from wet work.

## ✅ Bottom Line

Healthy cuticle care is simple: leave the seal intact, moisturize often, trim only loose hangnails and act quickly when pain, pus or persistent rash appears.
