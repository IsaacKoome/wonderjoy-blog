Razor bumps—also called pseudofolliculitis barbae—form when a cut hair curves back into the skin or grows sideways. They are especially common when hair is coarse and tightly curled, so Black men and anyone with curly facial or body hair may experience them frequently.

Inflamed bumps can leave persistent dark marks. Prevention therefore matters as much as treating the bump after it appears.

## 🪒 Do Not Shave Too Close

A perfectly smooth shave increases the chance that a short, sharp hair tip will re-enter the skin. Shave in the direction of growth, use light pressure, and avoid repeatedly passing over the same area.

Do not stretch the skin while shaving. When stretched skin relaxes, the cut hair retracts below the surface and can become trapped. An electric trimmer that leaves a small amount of stubble may be a better choice for recurring bumps.

## 🚿 Soften Hair Before the Razor Touches It

Shave after a warm shower or hold a warm, damp cloth over the area for several minutes. Apply a moisturizing shaving cream and let it sit briefly. Dry shaving creates more drag and micro-injury.

## 🔄 Use a Clean, Suitable Blade

A single-blade razor may reduce how close the hair is cut. If using a cartridge, avoid pressing down and replace it regularly. Rinse the blade after each stroke and store it somewhere dry.

An old blade tugs hair and encourages repeated passes. Never share razors.

## 🧊 Calm the Skin After Shaving

Rinse away residue and use a cool, damp cloth. Apply a fragrance-free moisturizer or non-stinging aftershave. Avoid alcohol-heavy products that burn; burning is irritation, not disinfection.

## 🚫 Do Not Dig Out the Hair

Do not squeeze bumps or use a needle to excavate hairs. That increases infection, scarring, and dark marks. A warm compress can encourage a superficial hair to release naturally.

Salicylic or glycolic acid may help some people, but start on non-shaving nights and never apply to raw skin. If bumps contain pus, spread, scar, or remain painful, see a dermatologist. Prescription treatment or laser hair reduction may be appropriate.

## ❓ Frequently Asked Questions

### Are razor bumps the same as acne?

No. They result from ingrown hairs, although folliculitis and acne can look similar.

### Should I shave every day?

If daily shaving triggers bumps, increasing the interval or using a trimmer may help.

### How do I fade the dark marks?

First prevent new bumps. Use sunscreen on exposed areas and discuss azelaic acid or other pigment treatment with a dermatologist if marks persist.

## ✨ Bottom Line

The closest shave is not always the healthiest shave. Softening the hair, following its growth direction, using light pressure, and moisturizing afterward can reduce both bumps and the dark marks they leave.
