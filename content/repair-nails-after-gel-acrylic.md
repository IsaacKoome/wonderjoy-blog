When enhancements come off, nails may feel thin, bendy, rough or tender. Gel, dip and acrylic systems can contribute through filing, repeated acetone exposure, adhesive chemicals and removal trauma—especially when product is peeled or drilled aggressively.

You cannot glue the nail plate back to its original thickness. Recovery means preventing further injury while stronger nail grows from the base.

## 🛑 First, Stop Peeling and Buffing

Do not pull off remaining product. Peeling can remove superficial layers of natural nail. If removal is incomplete, ask an experienced technician or dermatologist rather than repeatedly scraping.

Keep nails short enough that they do not flex painfully. File rough edges gently in one direction and avoid thinning the surface to hide ridges.

## 💧 Rehydrate Every Day

Massage a fragrance-free cream into hands, nails and cuticles after washing and at bedtime. A bland ointment over the nail folds at night can further reduce water loss. Moisturizer improves flexibility and comfort; it does not instantly replace lost nail layers.

## 🧤 Give Nails a Low-Trauma Holiday

Take at least one to two weeks without gel polish, and longer if nails remain fragile. Avoid glue-on nails used to hide damage, because removal can restart the cycle. Wear gloves for dishes and cleaning, but do not keep sweaty gloves on for hours.

Use mild soap or cleanser and dry around every nail fold.

## ☀️ If You Return to Gel, Reduce UV Exposure

UV nail lamps emit ultraviolet radiation. The AAD recommends broad-spectrum, water-resistant SPF 30+ on exposed hand skin or dark, opaque fingerless gloves before curing. LED lamps generally expose hands for less time than traditional UV lamps, but “LED” does not mean zero UV.

This protection addresses skin exposure, not the mechanical and chemical effects of the manicure itself.

## 📅 A Realistic Recovery Timeline

Improvement appears at the base first. Fingernails take months to fully replace themselves, while toenails take longer. Use monthly photos and watch the damage travel toward the free edge.

Seek care if you have persistent pain, swelling, pus, green-black discoloration, nail lifting, an itchy rash around several nails or no healthy growth from the base. Nail glue and acrylate allergy can affect areas beyond the fingertips, including eyelids or face.

## ❓ Frequently Asked Questions

### Will a nail hardener repair my nails?

Some products can temporarily reduce splitting, but others irritate or make nails too rigid. Ask a dermatologist if basic protection is not enough.

### Can I cover damage with another acrylic set?

Covering already weak nails can worsen the problem and hide infection or lifting.

### Should I take collagen or biotin?

Neither replaces careful removal and time. Evidence for biotin in healthy people is limited, and high doses can interfere with lab tests.

## ✅ Bottom Line

Post-manicure recovery is mostly subtraction: no peeling, no aggressive buffing, fewer wet-chemical exposures and consistent moisturizer while new nail grows.
