The skin around the eyes is thin and easy to irritate. Weather, over-cleansing, retinoids, acne treatments, fragrance, makeup and rubbing can leave it tight or flaky. Eczema and contact dermatitis can look similar, so persistent symptoms should not be diagnosed from a mirror alone.

Eye pain, light sensitivity, vision change, pus, blisters or severe swelling requires prompt professional care.

## ⏸️ Pause the Likely Irritants

For several days, stop optional eye products, scrubs, acids, retinoids, vitamin C and fragranced oils near the area. Avoid peeling flakes. If the problem improves, reintroduce only one product at a time after the skin is calm.

Remember indirect contact: shampoo, hair dye, nail products and fragrance can transfer to eyelids from hands or rinse water.

## 🫧 Cleanse With Less Contact

Use lukewarm water. If cleanser is needed, choose a mild fragrance-free formula and massage the rest of the face without scrubbing the eye contour. Pat dry with a clean soft towel.

## 💧 Apply a Small Amount of Moisturizer

Use clean hands and tap a rice-grain amount around the orbital bone, staying away from the eye and lash line unless the label specifically permits that area. More product is not always more hydrating; a thick layer can migrate into the eye or trigger milia in some people.

## ☀️ Protect Intact Skin Carefully

Once the area tolerates products, use broad-spectrum SPF 30+ around the eyes, plus sunglasses and a hat. A mineral sunscreen may sting less for some sensitive users, but any formula can irritate an individual. Flush with clean water if sunscreen enters the eye.

## 🚫 Do Not Self-Treat Eyelids With Strong Steroids

Medication close to the eye should be clinician-directed. Inappropriate or prolonged topical steroid use can cause skin and eye complications. Recurrent dryness may need patch testing for allergic contact dermatitis rather than a stronger moisturizer.

## 📓 Track Patterns for One Week

Note when tightness begins and what touched the area that day: cleanser, shampoo, makeup, nail products, eye drops or tissues. Change only one item at a time. A short, simple record is often more useful than repeatedly buying richer creams because it can reveal whether dryness follows a particular exposure.

## ❓ Quick Questions

**Can petroleum jelly help?** A clinician may recommend a bland occlusive for some people, but keep products out of the eye and stop if they worsen bumps or irritation.

**Why does moisturizer burn?** Damaged skin can sting, or the formula may contain an irritant or allergen. Persistent burning is a reason to stop.

**Should I exfoliate flakes?** No. Scrubbing creates more barrier damage.

## ✅ Bottom Line

Dry under-eyes usually need fewer products, less rubbing and a carefully tolerated moisturizer. Recurring redness, itch or swelling deserves professional evaluation.
