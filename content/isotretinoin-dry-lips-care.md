Very dry lips are one of the most common effects of isotretinoin because the medicine reduces oil production. Dryness often starts early and may continue throughout treatment. It does not mean you should change or stop your dose on your own.

Your prescribing clinician’s directions take priority. A simple, repeatable barrier routine usually works better than adding exfoliants or medicated lip products without advice.

Keep the balm where you will use it—at your bedside, desk and in your bag—without sharing it. If one formula burns, stop it and show the ingredient list to your pharmacist, dermatologist or prescribing clinician rather than cycling through strongly flavored alternatives.

## 💧 Protect Before Lips Crack

Apply a bland fragrance-free lip balm or ointment frequently—after eating, before outdoor exposure and in a generous layer at bedtime. Start early rather than waiting for painful fissures. Avoid flavors, fragrance, menthol, camphor, plumping ingredients and scrubs if they sting.

Do not bite or pull flakes. Contact your prescriber if cracking becomes severe, repeatedly bleeds, develops yellow crust or interferes with eating.

## 🫧 Make the Whole Routine Gentler

Isotretinoin can make facial skin dry and sensitive too. Wash with lukewarm water and a gentle cleanser, then pat rather than rub. Avoid facial scrubs, peels and unapproved acne actives; your prescriber can tell you which products to pause.

## ☀️ Protect Sensitive Skin Outdoors

Use broad-spectrum SPF 30+ on exposed skin and an SPF 30+ lip product, plus shade and a hat. Reapply outdoors. Ask your prescriber for a recommendation if every sunscreen stings; do not simply skip protection.

## 🚫 Avoid Extra Irritation

Do not wax facial skin during isotretinoin treatment, and follow your clinician’s timing for after treatment. Avoid cosmetic procedures unless the prescribing and treating clinicians agree. Tell your prescriber about eye dryness, nosebleeds, severe rash or any concerning physical or mood symptom.

Isotretinoin can cause severe birth defects. Follow the pregnancy-prevention and testing program provided by your clinical team exactly; skincare advice cannot replace it.

## ❓ Frequently Asked Questions

### Does dry skin mean my dose is too high?

Not necessarily. Dryness is expected, but only your prescriber should assess dose and side effects.

### Can I exfoliate peeling lips?

No. Scrubs and acids can worsen fragile, medicine-dried lips.

### Will drinking extra water fix it?

Normal hydration matters, but it does not replace a protective topical barrier.

## ✅ Bottom Line

Treat isotretinoin dryness proactively: bland lip protection, gentle face care and daily sun protection. Report severe or unusual symptoms instead of changing treatment yourself.
