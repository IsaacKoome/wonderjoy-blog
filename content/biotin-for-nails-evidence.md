Biotin appears on countless “hair, skin and nails” labels, often at doses far above daily nutritional needs. The marketing is much stronger than the research. Small uncontrolled studies reported harder nails in some people with brittle nails, but they do not prove that high-dose biotin helps everyone.

True biotin deficiency can affect hair, skin and nails, yet it is uncommon in people eating a varied diet. Brittle nails more often need an investigation of water exposure, manicure damage, skin disease, medication or health changes.

## 🧪 What the Evidence Shows

The NIH Office of Dietary Supplements says there is little scientific evidence supporting biotin supplements for hair, skin and nail health. The available brittle-nail studies were small and lacked placebo groups, so improvement could not be confidently attributed to biotin.

That is very different from evidence that a deficient person should replace a missing nutrient. A clinician can assess risk and decide whether testing or dietary review is appropriate.

## ⚠️ The Important Lab-Test Warning

High supplemental biotin can interfere with certain laboratory tests and cause falsely high or low results. The FDA has highlighted interference with some troponin tests used when evaluating heart attacks, and hormone tests can also be affected.

Tell every clinician and laboratory about supplements you take. Do not stop a prescribed product or guess a “washout” time without their instructions, because tests and doses differ.

Wear gloves for wet work, keep nails short, avoid peeling gel or acrylic product and moisturize after washing. These steps address common damage directly and do not interfere with blood tests.

## 🍽️ Choose Food Before Beauty Megadoses

Biotin is present in many foods, including cooked eggs, fish, meat, seeds, nuts and some vegetables. A balanced diet also supplies protein, iron, zinc and other nutrients involved in normal growth. More is not automatically better, and a supplement cannot compensate for a restrictive diet or unexplained weight loss.

## 🩺 When Brittle Nails Need Assessment

See a clinician for sudden change across many nails, spooning, deep horizontal grooves, pain, lifting, discoloration or brittleness accompanied by fatigue, hair loss, weight change or other symptoms. Do not self-treat suspected iron or thyroid problems with a beauty supplement.

## ❓ Frequently Asked Questions

### How long would biotin take to change nails?

Nails grow slowly, so any real change would require months. A quick result is unlikely.

### Is biotin harmless because it is water-soluble?

It can still cause clinically important lab-test interference, even if toxicity is uncommon.

### Should I buy a gummy marketed for nails?

Check the dose and other ingredients, then discuss it with a clinician—especially if you have upcoming blood tests, take medication, are pregnant or have health conditions.

## ✅ Bottom Line

Biotin is not a proven universal nail-growth shortcut. Protect nails, improve routine damage and investigate unexplained changes before adding a high-dose supplement.
