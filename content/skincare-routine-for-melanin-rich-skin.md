Melanin-rich skin does not need an entirely different set of skincare rules. It does, however, deserve a routine that takes **post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation** seriously. A pimple, harsh scrub, burn, or irritating product can leave a dark mark long after the original inflammation settles.

The best starting point is not a crowded shelf. It is a repeatable routine that cleanses gently, moisturizes well, protects from sunlight and visible light, and introduces treatment products slowly.

## 🌅 Step 1: Keep the Morning Routine Simple

A practical morning routine has three steps:

1. **Gentle cleansing:** Wash with a mild, fragrance-free cleanser, or simply rinse if your skin feels dry and was cleansed the night before.
2. **Moisturizing:** Use a non-comedogenic moisturizer suited to how your skin feels—not how it looks in one photograph.
3. **Sun protection:** Finish with broad-spectrum, water-resistant SPF 30 or higher.

Avoid chasing a “squeaky clean” feeling. Tightness after washing often signals that the cleanser or water temperature is too harsh. Repeated irritation can weaken the skin barrier and make uneven tone more noticeable.

## 🫧 Step 2: Choose a Cleanser That Does Not Strip

Gel, cream, and lotion cleansers can all work. The useful clues are the label and your skin’s response: look for “gentle,” “fragrance-free,” and “non-comedogenic.” After cleansing, your face should feel comfortable rather than dry, itchy, or burning.

If you wear makeup or water-resistant sunscreen, remove it thoroughly at night, but do not scrub. A cleansing balm or micellar water followed by a gentle cleanser may be enough.

## 💧 Step 3: Moisturize Even If You Are Oily

Oily skin can still become irritated or dehydrated. Skipping moisturizer may make acne treatments harder to tolerate. Lightweight lotions often suit oily skin, while creams containing ceramides may feel better on dry skin.

Apply moisturizer while the skin is slightly damp. If a product stings repeatedly, stop using it rather than assuming discomfort means it is working.

## ☀️ Step 4: Make Sunscreen Non-Negotiable

Melanin offers some natural UV protection, but it does not prevent sun damage or skin cancer. Sunlight and visible light can also deepen dark marks and melasma. Choose SPF 30 or higher, broad-spectrum protection, and water resistance when you will sweat or swim.

A **tinted sunscreen containing iron oxides** can help protect against visible light and may blend better than some untinted mineral formulas. The best sunscreen is one you can apply generously and reapply.

## 🌙 A Calm Evening Routine

At night, cleanse, apply one treatment if needed, and moisturize. Acne may respond to salicylic acid, benzoyl peroxide, adapalene, or prescription treatment. Uneven tone may respond to azelaic acid or a retinoid. Do not begin several actives at once; that makes irritation harder to identify.

Patch-test new products and begin two or three nights weekly. Increase only when your skin remains comfortable. Dark marks are usually improved by consistency, not by stronger peeling.

## 🚩 When to See a Dermatologist

Seek help for painful acne, spreading rashes, sudden pigment changes, scarring, or products that repeatedly cause swelling or burning. A dermatologist experienced with darker skin tones can distinguish cosmetic discoloration from a condition needing treatment.

## ❓ Frequently Asked Questions

### Does melanin-rich skin need sunscreen every day?

Yes. Sunscreen helps prevent UV damage and can keep existing dark marks from becoming darker.

### Is vitamin C required?

No. It can be useful, but cleanser, moisturizer, and sunscreen are the foundation. Add a serum only if it solves a clear concern and your skin tolerates it.

### How long should I test a routine?

Give a gentle routine several weeks. Acne and pigmentation treatments often need longer, while burning, swelling, or worsening irritation are reasons to stop sooner.

## ✨ Bottom Line

The best routine for melanin-rich skin is protective and boring in the best way: gentle cleansing, appropriate moisturizer, daily sunscreen, and only one carefully chosen treatment at a time.
