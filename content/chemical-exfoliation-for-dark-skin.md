Chemical exfoliants can smooth rough texture, unclog pores, and support treatment of uneven tone. They can also create irritation that leaves new dark marks. For melanin-rich skin, the question is not “Are acids allowed?” but “Which acid, how strong, and how often can this skin tolerate?”

More tingling and peeling do not mean better results.

## 🧪 AHA vs BHA: What Is the Difference?

**Alpha hydroxy acids (AHAs)** such as glycolic and lactic acid work mainly at the skin surface. They can help roughness and uneven-looking tone. Lactic acid is often considered a gentler starting option, although any AHA can irritate.

**Beta hydroxy acid (BHA)** usually means salicylic acid. It is oil-soluble, so it can enter pores and may suit blackheads and clogged bumps.

Choose based on the concern rather than stacking both immediately.

## 🐢 Start With One Low-Frequency Product

Patch-test first. Apply the exfoliant one evening weekly after gentle cleansing, then moisturize. If the skin remains comfortable for several weeks, you may increase according to the label.

Do not apply to broken, sunburned, or actively irritated skin. Stop when you see persistent burning, swelling, cracking, or new discoloration.

## 🚦Do Not Combine Every Active

Retinol, benzoyl peroxide, prescription acne medicines, scrubs, and exfoliating acids can all increase dryness. Using them together without a plan raises the chance of barrier damage.

Alternate nights or simplify the routine while introducing an acid. A plain moisturizer is not “canceling” the exfoliant; it is helping you tolerate treatment consistently.

## 📅 A Beginner Weekly Schedule

- **Monday:** Gentle cleanse, exfoliant, moisturizer
- **Tuesday–Thursday:** Gentle cleanse and moisturizer only
- **Friday:** Reassess for dryness; repeat only if comfortable
- **Every morning:** Moisturizer as needed and broad-spectrum SPF 30+

This is an example, not a target. Sensitive or dry skin may need less. Oily skin does not automatically need daily acid.

## ☀️ Sunscreen Protects Against a Setback

Exfoliation can increase sensitivity, while UV and visible light can worsen pigmentation. Use sunscreen every morning and reapply outdoors.

## ⚠️ At-Home Peels Are a Different Category

High-strength chemical peels can cause burns, permanent pigment changes, and scars. The FDA and AAD advise professional supervision. People with darker skin should seek a dermatologist experienced in treating their skin tone.

## ❓ Frequently Asked Questions

### Is glycolic acid bad for dark skin?

Not automatically. Strength, frequency, and individual sensitivity matter. Aggressive use is the bigger risk.

### Can I exfoliate away hyperpigmentation?

Exfoliation may support a broader plan, but overuse can create more pigment. Treat the cause and use sunscreen.

### Should an acid tingle?

A brief mild sensation can occur, but burning, pain, swelling, or lasting redness means you should rinse and stop.

## ✨ Bottom Line

Dark skin can use AHAs and BHAs, but restraint protects results. Pick one acid, begin infrequently, moisturize, and never treat irritation as proof of effectiveness.
