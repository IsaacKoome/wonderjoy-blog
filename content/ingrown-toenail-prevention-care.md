An ingrown toenail happens when the nail edge presses or grows into nearby skin, most often on the big toe. The area can become tender, red and swollen; infection may follow. Cutting deeper into the corner often makes the cycle worse.

Early, uncomplicated cases may settle with pressure reduction and careful foot care. Severe pain, pus or a high-risk foot needs professional help.

## ✂️ Trim Straight Across

Do not round down the side corners or cut the nail extremely short. Trim toenails straight across, leaving the corners visible beyond the skin. Use a toenail clipper, then smooth sharp edges gently with a file.

If the nail is already ingrown, the NHS advises letting it grow rather than cutting into it. Do not dig beneath it, perform “V cuts” or attempt bathroom surgery.

## 👟 Remove Pressure

Choose shoes with enough width and depth for toes to move. Tight, pointed shoes and repeated impact during running or football can press the nail into skin. Keep feet clean and dry, change damp socks and inspect toes after activity.

Wash intact skin gently and dry carefully.

## 🛁 Early Home Care

For a mild case, warm salty water may soften the surrounding skin; dry the foot for the rest of the day and wear wide shoes or sandals. Ask a pharmacist about pain relief and whether you need medical review.

Do not place sharp objects, cotton or dental floss beneath an inflamed nail unless a qualified clinician specifically instructs you. Avoid acids and strong exfoliants on broken skin.

Moisturize dry heels and the tops of feet, but keep thick cream out of moist toe webs and away from an open, weeping nail fold.

## 🚨 When to See a Clinician

Get medical care when home care is not helping, the toe is increasingly painful or swollen, pus appears, or you feel feverish or shivery. A podiatrist may remove part of a badly ingrown nail under local anesthetic.

If you have diabetes, poor circulation, reduced sensation or immune suppression, do not self-treat an ingrown toenail. Foot infections can become serious with fewer warning sensations.

Protect the tops of feet from sun in open shoes; sunscreen does not go into an infected nail fold.

## ❓ Frequently Asked Questions

### Will cutting a V in the nail make it grow away from skin?

No reliable evidence supports this. Cut straight across and avoid digging into corners.

### Can a pedicure fix it?

A salon should not perform a medical procedure on an inflamed toe. See a podiatrist or clinician.

### Why does it keep returning?

Very short curved trimming, tight shoes, repeated trauma and naturally curved nails can all contribute. Recurrent cases deserve podiatry advice.

## ✅ Bottom Line

Straight-across trimming, visible corners and roomy shoes prevent many ingrown nails. Once infected or recurrent, the safest solution is professional care—not deeper cutting.
