Recurring greasy flakes, itch and irritated patches can be signs of seborrheic dermatitis. It commonly affects oil-rich areas such as the scalp, eyebrows, ears and sides of the nose. Symptoms may improve and return; that does not mean you failed your routine.

Only a clinician can confirm the diagnosis because psoriasis, eczema, contact dermatitis and fungal infections can look similar. For an established mild case, however, a consistent wash-day plan is often more useful than constantly changing products.

## ðŸŒ€ Protect Curly and Coily Hair Lengths

Concentrate medicated shampoo on the scalp. After rinsing, use your normal conditioner on the hair lengths if needed. Detangle gently and limit direct heat during a flare. Heavy scalp oils may make residue and greasy scale harder to manage, although a little product on the lengths may still suit your hair.

Wash pillowcases, bonnets, scarves, combs and hats regularly. These steps do not cure seborrheic dermatitis, but they prevent stale product and scale from complicating the routine.

See a dermatologist if symptoms are severe, painful, spreading, causing hair loss, producing thick crusts or not improving after correctly used shampoo. Prescription antifungal shampoo, foam or cream may be appropriate. Short courses of anti-inflammatory medicine sometimes help, but steroid scalp products should not be improvised.

## â“ Frequently Asked Questions

### Is seborrheic dermatitis contagious?

No. It is a common inflammatory condition and cannot be caught from another person.

### Is it caused by a dirty scalp?

No. Washing habits can influence scale, but the condition is not evidence of poor hygiene.

### Can I cure it permanently?

There is no guaranteed permanent cure. Treatment aims to control symptoms and reduce flares.
