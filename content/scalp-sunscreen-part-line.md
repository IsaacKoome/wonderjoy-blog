The scalp is easy to forget until a part line feels hot and tender. Bald and thinning areas are clearly exposed, but tightly styled parts, braids and naturally wide part lines can burn too. UV protection also matters if a medication or coal-tar shampoo increases sun sensitivity.

## ðŸ§¢ A Hat Is the Simplest Option

Choose a wide-brimmed, tightly woven hat that shades the scalp, ears, face and neck. Baseball caps leave the ears and much of the neck exposed, so add sunscreen there. For long outdoor days, combine clothing, shade and sunscreen rather than relying on one layer.

Hair alone is uneven protection. Wet hair, sparse areas and precise part lines allow more UV to reach skin.

## â˜€ï¸ Apply SPF to Exposed Scalp Skin

Use broad-spectrum, water-resistant SPF 30 or higher on bald areas, thinning spots and the part. A lotion offers visible coverage; a sunscreen stick can trace a part; a spray or powder can be easier through hair but must still deliver enough product. Spray into your hand before applying near the face and avoid inhalation.

At the end of the day, shampoo or rinse as appropriate for the product and your hair type. Clean sunscreen from the forehead, ears and neck gently. If a scalp sunscreen repeatedly causes bumps, try a lighter non-comedogenic formula and rely more on a hat.

Use a mirror or ask someone to check areas you cannot see. A sore that does not heal, a changing mole, repeated bleeding or a new unusual growth needs medical evaluation. A dermatologist can examine the scalp through the hair.

## â“ Frequently Asked Questions

### Is powder sunscreen enough?

It can help with reapplication, but many people apply too little. Use a product and technique that provides even labeled coverage.

### Can sunscreen make hair greasy?

Yes. Sticks, lighter fluids and hats can reduce cosmetic inconvenience while preserving protection.

### Does dark skin need scalp sunscreen?

Yes. Melanin offers some natural protection but does not prevent all UV damage or skin cancer.
