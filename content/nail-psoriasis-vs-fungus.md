Nail psoriasis and fungal nail infection can both cause discoloration, thickening, crumbling and lifting. They can also occur together. That overlap makes self-diagnosis from a photo unreliable—and it explains why a treatment that helps one condition may do little for the other.

A dermatologist can examine all nails and skin, then test a clipping when fungus is possible.

## 🧩 Clues That Can Occur With Nail Psoriasis

Tiny dents called pits, lifting from the nail bed, yellow-brown “oil drop” discoloration, crumbling and buildup beneath the plate can occur. Psoriasis on the scalp, elbows, knees or elsewhere supports the possibility, but nail psoriasis can appear without obvious skin plaques.

Joint stiffness, swollen fingers or heel pain matters because nail psoriasis can accompany psoriatic arthritis. Mention these symptoms promptly.

## 🍄 Clues That Can Occur With Fungus

Fungal infection often begins at the end or side of a toenail and gradually causes yellow-brown color, thickness and crumbly edges. Athlete’s foot between the toes may be present. However, these are clues—not confirmation.

Do not scrape buildup from beneath a lifted nail. That can increase separation and infection risk.

## 🧪 Testing Changes the Plan

For suspected fungus, a clinician may send a nail clipping or scraping to a laboratory before prescribing prolonged medicine. For psoriasis, the distribution of pits, skin findings and history guide diagnosis. Treatment may be topical, injected or systemic depending on severity.

Over-the-counter antifungal cream for athlete’s foot does not reliably penetrate a thick nail plate. Conversely, steroid treatment used without medical guidance could worsen an unrecognized infection.

## 🧤 Protect Nails While Awaiting Care

Keep nails short, clean and dry. Wear gloves for wet work, leave cuticles alone and moisturize hands after washing. For feet, use breathable shoes and avoid sharing clippers.

## 🚩 Get Earlier Help When Risk Is Higher

Seek prompt review for pain, pus, bleeding, rapid lifting or difficulty walking. People with diabetes, poor circulation or immune suppression need early foot assessment.

A new dark nail streak is not a standard sign to label “fungus” or “psoriasis.” It deserves dermatology review to exclude melanoma.

Sun protection on hands helps exposed skin, especially around UV manicure use, but does not treat either nail condition.

## ❓ Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I have psoriasis in only one nail?

It is possible, but a single changed nail has several potential causes and needs examination.

### Is nail psoriasis contagious?

No. Psoriasis is inflammatory, not an infection.

### How quickly will nails clear?

Slowly. Even when treatment controls the cause, damaged nail must grow out over months.

## ✅ Bottom Line

Pitting may point toward psoriasis and gradual edge thickening may fit fungus, but neither is diagnostic. Testing protects you from months of the wrong treatment.
