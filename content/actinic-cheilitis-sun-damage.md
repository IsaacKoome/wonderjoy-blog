Most chapped lips improve when irritants stop and the barrier is protected. **Actinic cheilitis** is different: it is a chronic sun-related change, usually affecting the lower lip, and is considered precancerous. It can be subtle, so it should not be diagnosed—or dismissed—from a photograph.

The aim is not to make every dry lip frightening. It is to recognize a persistent pattern that deserves an in-person examination.

Compare the area over time without picking it, and note whether it repeatedly returns in exactly the same location.

## 🔎 Signs That Need Attention

Actinic cheilitis may cause lasting dryness, scale, pale or discolored areas, tenderness and a blurred border between the lip and surrounding skin. A focal thick area, ulcer, nodule, repeated bleeding or increasing pain raises more concern for progression.

Risk rises with cumulative ultraviolet exposure, fair skin, outdoor work and increasing age, but anyone with a changing lip lesion should be assessed. Ordinary balm cannot determine whether abnormal cells are present.

## 🩺 Do Not Self-Treat a Persistent Patch

Arrange a dermatologist or qualified clinician visit when one area remains rough despite two to three weeks of gentle care, or sooner for ulceration, a lump or bleeding. The clinician may examine the whole lip and sometimes take a biopsy. Treatment depends on the extent and findings.

Keep cleansing gentle while waiting. Do not sand the patch with a scrub or apply facial acids.

## ☀️ Build Daily Lip Sun Protection

Use a broad-spectrum, water-resistant SPF 30+ lip product outdoors and reapply at least every two hours and after eating or drinking. Add a wide-brimmed hat and shade. This helps prevent additional exposure but does not erase established actinic cheilitis.

A facial mineral sunscreen can protect skin around the mouth. Only apply it directly to lips if the label permits that use.

## ❓ Frequently Asked Questions

### Is actinic cheilitis lip cancer?

It is a precancerous sun-damaged change, not automatically cancer. Medical review matters because some cases can progress.

### Can it affect darker skin tones?

Yes. Risk patterns differ, but persistent or changing lip lesions deserve examination in every skin tone.

### Will SPF make an existing patch disappear?

Sun protection helps prevent further exposure; it does not replace diagnosis or treatment.

## ✅ Bottom Line

A persistent rough lower-lip area is not something to scrub away. Protect it from sun and arrange an examination, especially if it thickens, bleeds or ulcerates.
