Despite the name, a hangnail is a small torn piece of **skin** at the side of a nail. Dryness, frequent washing, biting, picking and cuticle cutting make them more likely. Pulling one can extend the tear into living skin and create a painful route for infection.

A minor hangnail usually needs clean tools, moisturizer and patience—not surgery or harsh antiseptic.

## ✂️ Trim, Never Rip

Wash your hands with mild cleanser and lukewarm water. If the skin is stiff, soak the fingertip briefly, then pat dry. With clean, sharp nail clippers, snip only the loose dead skin close to its base. Do not cut into attached skin.

If it bleeds, apply gentle pressure with clean gauze. Do not chew it off or dig underneath the nail fold.

## 💧 Protect the Skin Barrier

Apply a fragrance-free cream after every handwash, including around the nail folds. A thin layer of plain petrolatum at bedtime can reduce friction and water loss. Cover a tender spot with a small clean bandage during tasks that may snag it, changing the bandage if wet or dirty.

## 🧤 Prevent the Next Tear

Wear suitable gloves for dishes and cleaning, keep nails at a manageable length and stop cutting attached cuticles. File sharp nail corners that catch on fabric. If biting or picking happens during stress, place moisturizer or a bandage where your fingers can feel it and substitute a fidget object.

UV protection matters for the skin on hands, particularly if you use manicure lamps or spend time outdoors. It does not directly treat a hangnail, so keep sunscreen away from an open cut if the label warns against broken skin.

## 🚨 Watch for Paronychia

Increasing redness, warmth, swelling and throbbing beside the nail may signal infection. Pus, an open sore, red streaking, fever or rapidly worsening pain needs medical advice. Do not puncture a swollen nail fold yourself.

People with diabetes, poor circulation or reduced immunity should seek care earlier. Repeated swelling around several nails may also reflect irritant dermatitis, allergy or chronic paronychia rather than a single hangnail.

## ❓ Frequently Asked Questions

### Should I use alcohol or hydrogen peroxide?

Routine harsh antiseptics can irritate healing skin. Gentle washing and a clean covering are usually enough for a small uncomplicated tear; ask a pharmacist or clinician about wound care if unsure.

### Can I push my cuticles back to prevent hangnails?

No. Forceful pushing can injure the protective seal and create more rough skin.

### Why do I keep getting them in winter?

Cold air, indoor heating and repeated handwashing increase dryness. Reapply cream rather than waiting for cracking.

## ✅ Bottom Line

Soften, clean and clip only the loose piece. Moisturize after washing, protect hands from wet work and get help for spreading inflammation or pus.
