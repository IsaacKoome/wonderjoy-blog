Removing eye makeup should dissolve product, not sandpaper it away. Repeated rubbing can irritate eyelid skin, worsen puffiness and break lashes. The safest routine uses clean hands, enough remover and patience.

## 🧼 Begin With Clean Hands

Wash and rinse your hands before touching the eye area. Do not share mascara, liner or eye tools. If you develop an eye infection, stop cosmetics and discard products used around the time symptoms began according to medical guidance.

## ⏳ Hold, Then Wipe

Saturate a soft pad with a remover intended for eye use. With the eye closed, hold it in place briefly to loosen makeup. Wipe outward with light pressure. Use a fresh section for the next pass and a separate pad for the other eye when infection is a concern.

Never scrape the waterline with a fingernail or cotton swab.

## 🫧 Follow With Gentle Cleansing

If residue remains on surrounding skin, use a mild fragrance-free cleanser and lukewarm water. Avoid pushing cleanser into the eye or repeatedly washing until the skin squeaks.

## 🗓️ Keep Makeup Hygienic

Close containers, wash reusable tools and do not add water or saliva to dried mascara. The FDA recommends discarding mascara three months after purchase. Avoid applying or removing eye makeup in a moving vehicle because an applicator can scratch the eye.

In the morning, use carefully placed sunscreen around intact eye-area skin.

## 👁️ Protect the Eye, Not Just the Skin

Never force remover beneath the lash line or use fingernails to scrape away liner. If a product enters the eye, rinse with clean water rather than adding another cosmetic to neutralize it. Stop using any eye cosmetic associated with burning, swelling or discharge. Persistent pain, light sensitivity, blurred vision or a scratched-eye sensation warrants prompt professional assessment.

Waterproof makeup naturally takes longer to dissolve. Give the remover more contact time instead of increasing pressure, and use a fresh section of the pad for each pass. That small change reduces repeated rubbing and keeps loosened pigment from being dragged back across the eyelid.

## ❓ Quick Questions

**Is an oil cleanser safe for eyes?** Only use products labeled for that area and follow directions; oils can still blur or irritate eyes.

**Should makeup wipes be the daily method?** They can encourage repeated rubbing and may leave cleanser behind.

**What if remover always burns?** Stop using it and choose another properly labeled product; persistent irritation needs care.

## ✅ Bottom Line

Soak, hold and wipe gently. Clean hands, fresh products and minimal friction protect both eyelid skin and the eye itself.
