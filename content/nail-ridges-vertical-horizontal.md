Lines in a nail can look alarming because social media often links every ridge to a deficiency. In reality, the **direction, depth, number of nails involved and timing** matter more than a single close-up photograph.

Fine lengthwise ridges are common, especially with age or dryness. Deep grooves running across the nail—often called Beau’s lines—mean growth slowed or stopped for a period. Neither pattern should be diagnosed from appearance alone.

## ↕️ Vertical Ridges

Vertical ridges run from cuticle to tip. Mild ridging across several nails can be a normal variation and often becomes more noticeable over time. Dryness, repeated wet work and surface trauma can emphasize the texture.

Do not aggressively buff ridges flat. The visible plate is already thin, and repeated buffing may make it weaker. Keep nails short, file gently in one direction and moisturize the nail and surrounding skin.

## ↔️ Horizontal Grooves

A deep groove that crosses the width of one nail may follow direct injury to that nail’s growth center. Similar grooves appearing across several nails at roughly the same level can follow a significant illness, high fever, chemotherapy, severe stress or another event that temporarily affected growth.

The groove itself cannot tell you the cause. If you remember a major illness months earlier, the line may simply be growing outward. If grooves recur or you cannot explain them, show a clinician.

## 🧼 Protect the New Growth

Use gloves for prolonged wet work and avoid picking the skin at the nail base. Cuticles help seal the area where new nail emerges; pushing or cutting them can create inflammation and additional ridging.

Wash gently, dry well and avoid scraping beneath the nail with sharp tools.

## 🚩 Changes Worth Checking Promptly

Arrange medical review if ridges come with nail pain, swelling, lifting, pitting, crumbling, spoon-shaped nails, color change or a new dark band. Nail pitting may occur with psoriasis, eczema or alopecia areata. Spoon-shaped nails can be associated with iron deficiency, but laboratory testing—not self-supplementing—should guide treatment.

If the skin on the hands is sun-exposed while you monitor nail changes, protect it as you would the face. This is especially relevant around UV manicure lamps.

## 📷 Track, Do Not Obsess

Remove polish and photograph the same nail monthly in neutral light. Note illness, injury, manicures and new medication dates. Because nails grow slowly, a ridge moving steadily toward the tip supports an older, time-limited event; a change forming repeatedly near the cuticle needs assessment.

## ❓ Frequently Asked Questions

### Are vertical ridges a vitamin deficiency?

Usually not by themselves. Persistent or dramatic changes with other symptoms deserve evaluation rather than guessing a supplement.

### Can moisturizer remove a ridge?

It cannot erase the formed plate, but it can reduce dryness and breakage while new nail grows.

### How fast do fingernails grow out?

Complete replacement takes months, so improvement is gradual. Toenails take longer.

## ✅ Bottom Line

Fine vertical ridges are often harmless. Deep horizontal grooves record a period of interrupted growth and may need investigation when unexplained, recurrent or present with other nail changes.
