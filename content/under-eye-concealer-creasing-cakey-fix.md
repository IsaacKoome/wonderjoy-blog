Every under-eye has natural folds and movement, so perfectly crease-free concealer all day is an unrealistic promise. Caking becomes more noticeable when skin is dry, too much product is applied, heavy layers are mixed or powder settles into lines.

The fix is usually less makeup and better timing—not aggressive exfoliation.

## 💧 Prep With a Thin Moisturizer Layer

On clean skin, tap a small amount of a tolerated moisturizer along the orbital bone. Wait several minutes before makeup. Thick eye cream immediately beneath concealer can make it slide, while skipping moisturizer can highlight flakes.

## 🎨 Correct Only Where Needed

Place tiny dots on the darkest areas rather than painting a thick triangle. Blend outward with a clean fingertip, brush or damp sponge. Let one thin layer settle before deciding whether you need another.

If darkness is mostly a hollow shadow, adding opaque concealer everywhere may create texture without removing the shadow.

## 🧽 Use Less Powder

Before setting, look up and gently tap out product collected in folds. Apply a very small amount of finely milled powder only where movement is greatest. “Baking” can make dry skin look heavier.

## 🫧 Remove It Without Scrubbing

At night, hold an eye-safe remover on the closed eye to dissolve makeup, then gently cleanse surrounding skin. Repeated rubbing can worsen dryness and puffiness.

## ☀️ Do Not Forget UV Protection

Use a tolerated broad-spectrum SPF 30+ before makeup and allow it to set. Tinted sunscreen may reduce how much concealer you want, but apply sunscreen in its recommended amount rather than treating concealer as the protective layer.

## 🚫 When Makeup Is Not the Main Problem

Pause makeup if the eyelids burn, itch, swell or scale. That pattern may be contact dermatitis rather than poor prep. Eye pain, discharge or vision change requires medical care.

## 🧪 Change One Variable at a Time

When troubleshooting, do not replace your moisturizer, concealer, powder and sponge on the same morning. First test a smaller amount of your existing concealer. Next try less powder, then adjust prep if needed. Photograph the result in the same light after one hour and again later in the day. This shows whether the product separates immediately or only after oil, movement or dryness develops.

Deep expression lines will still move, and some creasing is normal. The realistic goal is comfortable, even-looking makeup—not a rigid under-eye surface. Repeatedly adding powder to erase every line often creates the very cakiness you are trying to prevent.

## ❓ Quick Questions

**Should I exfoliate the under-eye?** Usually no. Acids and scrubs can create more dryness and irritation.

**Is primer necessary?** Not always. More layers can increase pilling; test one change at a time.

**Can concealer permanently hide bags?** It can reduce color contrast, but structural bulges and shadows remain.

## ✅ Bottom Line

Moisturize lightly, use less concealer, tap out folds and powder sparingly. Natural movement means a little creasing is normal.
