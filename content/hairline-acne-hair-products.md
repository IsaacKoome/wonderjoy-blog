If bumps form mainly along the upper forehead, temples or back of the neck, look at what touches those areas. Oils from pomades, conditioners, styling waxes and sprays can clog pores. Dermatologists call these product-triggered breakouts **acne cosmetica**.

The fix is not simply a stronger acne treatment. First reduce the repeated exposure that keeps refilling the pores.

Residue transfers to pillowcases, bonnets, scarves, hats, headbands, helmet padding and headphones. Launder or wipe these items according to their care instructions. Avoid sharing hair tools or hats while you have an unexplained scalp rash.

Friction and trapped sweat can add acne mechanica. Use required protective gear, but clean it and remove it when no longer needed.

## âšª Treat the Skin According to the Bump

Salicylic acid can help whiteheads and tiny clogged bumps. Start two or three times weekly and increase only if tolerated. Do not combine it immediately with scrub beads, peel pads and a harsh acne wash.

Inflamed facial acne may respond to benzoyl peroxide or adapalene, but both require label directions and can irritate. Benzoyl peroxide may bleach hair and fabrics. Keep any treatment on skin rather than spreading it through the hair.

## ðŸš© When It Might Not Be Acne

Very itchy identical bumps, marked scaling, burning after dye, blisters, crusting or patchy hair loss suggest another cause. Stop the newest suspect product and seek medical advice. See a dermatologist if the area does not improve after about six weeks without likely clogging products.

## â“ Frequently Asked Questions

### Do bangs cause acne?

Not automatically. Oil, sweat, product residue and friction under bangs may contribute.

### Must I stop every hair product?

No. Start with products that touch the breakout area and change one variable at a time.

### Should I wash my face after styling?

Remove visible residue, but repeated full washing can irritate. Preventing contact is easier.
