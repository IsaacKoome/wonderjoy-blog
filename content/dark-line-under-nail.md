A brown or black line running from the base to the tip of a nail is called longitudinal melanonychia. Pigment can arise for several reasons, including normal variation, trauma, medication, a mole in the nail matrix or—rarely—melanoma.

People with darker skin tones may naturally have pigment bands in more than one nail. That fact should never be used to dismiss a **new, changing or irregular** band. Nail melanoma is uncommon, but delayed diagnosis can be serious.

## 🚩 Arrange a Dermatology Check

The American Academy of Dermatology advises having a new or changing dark streak examined. Pay particular attention when a band is widening, has uneven color or borders, appears in one nail without explanation, or pigment extends onto the surrounding skin.

Other concerning changes include nail splitting, lifting, bleeding, a lump beneath the nail or persistent pain. You do not need to decide whether the line is cancer before making an appointment.

## 📷 Document, but Do Not Delay

Remove polish and take a clear photograph in neutral light with the date. Photograph again only if the appointment is not immediate. A bruise from trauma often moves toward the tip as the nail grows, while pigment produced at the nail matrix continues from the base—but this observation cannot safely rule out melanoma.

Do not file, bleach, burn or cover the line with another artificial nail while waiting for assessment.

## 🩺 What a Clinician May Ask

Be ready to discuss when the line appeared, whether it changed, recent injury, medication, family melanoma history and pigment changes elsewhere. The dermatologist may use dermoscopy and, when necessary, test tissue from the nail unit. A photograph or AI scan cannot replace that examination.

## 🛡️ Care for the Nail While You Wait

Keep it at a practical length, avoid picking the cuticle and protect it from repeated impact. Moisturize surrounding skin if dry, but understand that cream cannot remove pigment produced within the nail.

## ☀️ Include Nails in Skin Checks

Check fingernails and toenails when you examine your skin. Look beneath polish occasionally and protect the backs of hands and exposed feet from UV. Sunscreen reduces skin UV exposure; it does not prevent every nail melanoma or make an existing band safe to ignore.

## ❓ Frequently Asked Questions

### Are dark lines normal in dark skin?

Multiple stable bands can be a normal pigment pattern, but new or changing lines still require professional review.

### Could it just be a bruise?

Yes, trauma can cause blood under a nail. If there was no clear injury, the mark does not grow out or it has warning signs, get it examined.

### Should I wait to see whether it widens?

No. A new or changing band is a reason to schedule a dermatologist now; photographs can support, not replace, the visit.

## ✅ Bottom Line

Most nail pigment is not melanoma, but no online checklist can safely make that call. A new, widening or irregular dark nail band deserves a dermatology examination.
